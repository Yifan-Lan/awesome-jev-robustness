# Contributing

Add one line in the section that fits, in a pull request. The list covers the robustness of decision models: TypeSafe's Jev first, because it shipped first and most studies target it, and also Clef, Decision-1, OpenAI Decisions, Laya, Kev and open Jev-style checkpoints when a study reports the same kind of finding about them. The list is deliberately narrow: an entry has to report **a finding about change or failure**, that is, how a decision model's answer or probability moves with wording, options, order, repetition, language, planted text or unanswerable input, or where its probability is miscalibrated (over- or under-confident, confidence that does not drop on unanswerable items, calibration that does not transfer). A score is not a finding. Say which model and version the finding is about. The following go in `data/task_benchmarks.tsv` and stay off the README page, however carefully they were run: accuracy, cost or latency comparisons against other models; general evaluations and leaderboards; domain benchmarks that report an ECE or Brier next to an accuracy without a miscalibration finding; and studies of Jev *as* a detector, judge or router that measure its capability rather than its sensitivity. Surveys and meta-reviews go in the write-ups section. Keep the format of the lines around it:

    - [name](url) - What was measured, on how many items, and the headline number.

An entry needs all three of these, or a link to a page that has them:

- what it measured about a decision model's behavior, not just that it uses one;
- which model and version it hit (`jev-1.13.0`, `jev-1.14.0`, `jev-latest` on a given date, a gateway name, or the open checkpoint and revision);
- how many items were run.

Things that do not go here: tools, SDKs, applications, general project directories, and open reproductions or new models that report accuracy against quoted Jev numbers but no robustness measurement of their own. There are good lists for those, linked at the bottom of the README.

Descriptions are plain statements of what the authors report. No adjectives about how impressive the result is. If the authors state a caveat (small sample, labels generated by another model, no baseline), the description should carry it.

If an entry describes your work incorrectly, open an issue or a pull request with the correction and a pointer to where the right number is. If you would rather not be listed, say so and the entry will be removed.

Entries that could not be confirmed to contain measurements are kept in `data/candidates_unconfirmed.tsv` rather than the README. Confirming one of them, with a link to the numbers, is a welcome pull request.
